# Portraits and Faces

Use for headshots, busts, profile pictures, expressive faces, and stylized portraits.

## Plan first
1. Decide view: front, three-quarter, profile, or tilted; choose a style and expression.
2. Establish the head's outer shape, centerline, eye line, and jaw before adding features.
3. Place features relative to the head, not independently on the canvas.
4. Decide hair mass and silhouette early; hair is a large shape, not a collection of random strands.
5. Keep the background quiet unless it carries meaning.

## Face construction
- Treat the head as a volume. The centerline bends or shifts with head rotation.
- Place eyes along a shared perspective line; the far eye is usually narrower in a three-quarter view.
- Keep nose and mouth aligned to the centerline and expression.
- Build ears from the eye-to-nose region as a rough guide, then adapt to style.
- Use a few large hair shapes first, then add only a few strands or highlights.
- Use eyebrow angle, eyelid shape, mouth corners, and head tilt together to express emotion.

## Avoid common failures
- Do not draw two identical eyes at equal width when the head is turned.
- Avoid features floating independently from the skull or jaw.
- Avoid excessive tiny strokes before the face reads at small size.
- Use deliberate asymmetry in hair, brows, expression, and head tilt; do not introduce random facial misalignment.

## Verification
Check the silhouette, gaze direction, feature alignment, expression readability, hair/face overlap, and crop. Render at the intended display size as well as full size.
