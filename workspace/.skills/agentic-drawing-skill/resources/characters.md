# Character Illustration

Use for full-body characters, action poses, costumes, creatures, and character concept art rendered as SVG or other structured geometry.

## Plan first
1. Identify subject, role/personality, pose, viewpoint, style, and intended use or size.
2. Define a readable silhouette and line of action before adding costume details.
3. Establish head-to-body proportions, torso direction, pelvis, limb placement, and weight/support.
4. Choose a small palette: base, shadow, highlight, and accent.
5. Layer from back to front: far limbs/equipment, torso, near limbs, head/hair, clothing details, face, foreground effects.

## Pose and anatomy
- Build the pose from simple masses and joint landmarks before drawing contours.
- Check that shoulders, hips, elbows, knees, wrists, and ankles connect plausibly.
- Make the feet or other support points agree with gravity and the pose.
- For dynamic poses, use a clear line of action; avoid making every limb parallel to the torso.
- Hands and feet can be simplified when small, but should still point and attach coherently.

## Break symmetry deliberately
Perfect symmetry often makes a character look like a mannequin. Unless the design calls for symmetry:
- Tilt the head slightly and offset the shoulders and hips.
- Give the arms different angles or tasks; avoid mirror-image hands.
- Shift weight onto one leg, bend the other, and vary foot direction.
- Vary hair clumps, clothing folds, accessories, and silhouette on each side.
- Offset facial features subtly only when appropriate to the style; keep the eyes intentional, not accidentally misaligned.
- Use small rotations and controlled asymmetry, not random jitter. Every difference should support pose, personality, or material.

## Verification
Check silhouette at thumbnail size, limb attachment, overlap order, palette contrast, clipping, and whether the pose communicates the intended action. Do not add fine detail until the silhouette and pose read clearly.
