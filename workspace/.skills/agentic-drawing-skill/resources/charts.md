# Charts and Infographics

Use for quantitative charts, comparisons, timelines, and information-heavy visuals. Accuracy and legibility matter more than decoration.

## Choose the representation
- Comparison across categories: bar chart.
- Trend over ordered time: line chart.
- Part-to-whole: stacked bar or pie only when categories are few and meaningful.
- Distribution: histogram or dot/box plot where suitable.
- Sequence over time: timeline.
- Relationships between concepts: diagram, not a chart.
If the data is unavailable, do not invent values. Ask for it or label any illustrative data clearly.

## Plan first
1. Identify the takeaway the chart should communicate.
2. Preserve the supplied values, units, labels, and ordering.
3. Choose scales that do not distort the message; show a zero baseline for bars unless there is a justified alternative.
4. Label axes and units; include a legend only when needed.
5. Use colour consistently and do not rely on colour alone to distinguish categories.

## Verification
Check that plotted values match the source, axes and units are correct, labels are legible, the scale is honest, and the title reflects the actual data. Avoid decorative effects that obscure comparisons.
